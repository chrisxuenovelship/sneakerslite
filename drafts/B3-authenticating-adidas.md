A marketplace that treats adidas as one authentication category will miss the point. A suede terrace shoe, a knit runner and a moulded foam slide present different surfaces, labels and counterfeit risks. The useful question is not whether a vendor handles the brand. It is which models it can judge, from which images, and what happens when the evidence is incomplete.

## Why adidas Is Targeted

The terrace revival has put Samba, Gazelle, Spezial and Campus 00s back into everyday resale searches. Their familiar shapes and overlapping colourways create demand for careful model identification as well as authentication. A genuine-looking three-stripe side panel is not enough when the stitching, panel proportions and materials differ between silhouettes and releases.

YEEZY is a different case. The 350 V2, 700, Slide and Foam Runner have long attracted counterfeit production and secondary-market attention. Their varied construction means there is no single YEEZY tell: a knit upper, a layered runner and a one-piece foam form demand different checks. Resale interest and pricing can shift quickly, so a platform's intake mix can change before its inspection checklist does.

Ultraboost and NMD add knit and foam combinations to the catalogue. Collaborations add another complication: a distinctive colourway or unusual material is not proof of authenticity, and an unfamiliar detail is not proof of a fake. These are observations about where resale demand and inspection complexity meet, not a claim that every named model is currently covered by any particular system.

## Where the Tells Are

**Construction.** On three-stripe shoes, compare stripe spacing and angle against the relevant model and release, then look at heel-tab stitching and panel alignment. On a Samba, the T-toe overlay stitching and its junction with the vamp deserve a clear close-up. On a Gazelle, suede panel seams are more useful than a distant side profile. A single irregular stitch can be a factory variation or wear; a repeated mismatch across regions is more informative.

**Knit and foam construction.** Boost pellet size and pattern uniformity, Primeknit tension and pattern, and the weave around a YEEZY 350 V2's 'SPLY-350' stripe are potential comparative signals. On a Foam Runner, mould seam position and the edges of its openings are worth documenting from more than one angle. These details must be judged against the specific release: changing one material or production method can make an old reference image misleading.

**Labels and packaging.** Ask for a legible size tag showing its format, article number and factory code, alongside any 'Made in' marking and date code. The box label layout must be checked against the appropriate era, not against an assumed universal adidas template. A sticker QR code can be captured and assessed with the surrounding typography and layout; a scannable code by itself does not authenticate the pair. Keep the shoe and box evidence together in the case record so a plausible box cannot quietly substitute for a mismatch on the shoe.

**Materials.** Suede nap, Boost translucency and squeak, Primeknit elasticity and EVA foam texture are useful inspection prompts, but not all are image evidence. A photograph can show surface texture and sometimes translucency; it cannot establish squeak or elasticity by touch. If a material question depends on handling the item, route it to an inspector rather than presenting a visual result as a physical test.

## What Our Pipeline Checks

SneakersLite's pipeline starts with image standardisation: pose estimation and OpenCV-based normalisation bring submitted views to a consistent orientation and scale. A Samba's T-toe image and a Gazelle side view then become more comparable to the appropriate reference views. Standardisation cannot restore a missing label photo or turn a blurred seam into reliable evidence; submission quality still matters.

Visual feature analysis examines colour, texture and shape ratio across those standardised images. For adidas submissions, this can bring attention to stripe geometry, panel proportions, suede surfaces, knit patterns or the outline of a moulded foam shoe, depending on the model. A texture difference is a signal to interpret alongside the rest of the evidence, not a verdict in isolation. Lighting, wear and camera compression can alter how a material appears.

Tag and label authentication uses OCR on barcodes, QR codes and typography. That layer can compare readable tag and box-label details with the expected format for the relevant model and production context. The visual and label signals combine into a confidence score with a verification breakdown. Cases below the threshold route to human review, and reviewed cases feed back into training. The breakdown lets an operator distinguish a strong visual match with weak label evidence from a submission that is weak across both.

The same principle applies within adidas: training for a terrace shoe does not automatically establish coverage for a knit runner or moulded slide.

## Coverage Today

Coverage is model-level, not brand-level. Saying 'adidas covered' is not an actionable catalogue commitment: a platform needs to know which models are in scope, which variants require further review and how newly released pairs will be handled. The current model list for adidas is disclosed on request and expands monthly. We do not treat demand for Samba, Gazelle or YEEZY as evidence that every release in those families is covered.

New models launch at a conservative threshold. Sub-threshold cases go to human review, and as reviewed submissions feed training, the review rate falls over weeks. For an operator, the meaningful coverage discussion therefore includes both the model list and its maturity. Ask what happens to a listing when its exact article number or colourway has not yet accumulated enough reference evidence.

## For Operators

**Define the capture set by construction.** A standard side, heel, tag and box sequence is a useful starting point, but knit-based uppers photograph differently from smooth leather or suede. Ask whether your vendor needs additional close-ups of weave tension and the stripe region on a 350 V2, and whether low light or compression obscures those signals. For a Foam Runner, ask which angles expose the mould seam without hiding the openings.

**Separate category demand from coverage.** YEEZY resale volatility can alter which models reach your intake desk. Request the current model-level list, the route for an uncovered variant and the process for adding a newly active release. Do not use a brand badge on a listing page to imply that every item received the same depth of inspection.

**Decide how uncertainty appears in your workflow.** Ask for the confidence score and verification breakdown, then define when a case pauses for human review, what evidence the reviewer sees and when a seller receives an update. A label mismatch and an unreadable label are different operational problems; neither should be collapsed into a silent pass.
